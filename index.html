💧 Water Clarity

Water Clarity Scale (provisional)

Estimated for each spot from recent offshore waves, wind and rain, the latest satellite water-clarity reading, and what is typical for the time of year. Gulf-side spots are called Muddy after big swells and rough weather. Tap a spot's water badge to see the reasoning. Your water reports are saved with each log entry so the model can be checked against real conditions later; they do not change the estimates yet.

  • Crystal: Gin clear. Sight-fishing conditions; fish and lures are visible a long way.
  • Clear: Clean water. Good visibility; natural presentations work well.
  • Fair: Slightly stained. Visible at moderate range; a good all-around condition.
  • Murky: Stained or milky. Fish rely more on scent and vibration; bolder baits and lures help.
  • Muddy: Churned up or heavily discolored. Visibility is close to nothing; fish scent, noise and structure.

💨 24-Hour Wind Forecast

Angling Wind Scale Matrix

Wind dynamics drastically change casting mechanics, water presentation, and fish comfort zones. The engine now tags wind streams globally using these four targeted boundaries:

  • 0 – 3 mph (Still): Grease calm. Water surface behaves like a mirror. High visibility makes shallow-water fish highly skittish; stealth is required, or target deeper zones.
  • 4 – 10 mph (Light): The Ideal Ripple. Breaks up the fish’s visual window to mask your profile while maintaining perfect boat control and effortless casting.
  • 11 – 16 mph (Moderate): Active Chop. Demands heavier jigs or weedless configurations. Concentrates predator ambush points along windward shoreline points.
  • 17+ mph (Unpleasantly Strong): Blown Out. Destroys visibility due to muddy substrate churning, causes severe drift vectors, and presents small-craft safety hazards.
